4    A BILL to amend and reenact § 2.2-3704 of the Code of
5    Virginia, relating to Virginia Freedom of Information
6    Act; response to requests for public records; charges;
7    public utility account numbers.

9         Patron-- Coyner
10        ----------
11   Be it enacted by the General Assembly of Virginia:
12   1. That § 2.2-3704 of the Code of Virginia is amended
13   and reenacted as follows:
14   § 2.2-3704. Public records to be open to inspection;
15   procedure for requesting records and responding to
16   request; charges; transfer of records for storage, etc.
17   A. Except as otherwise specifically provided by law, all
18   public records shall be open to citizens of the
19   Commonwealth, representatives of newspapers and magazines
20   with circulation in the Commonwealth, and representatives
21   of radio and television stations broadcasting in or into
22   the Commonwealth during the regular office hours of the
23   custodian of such records. Access to such records shall
24   not include access to the account numbers of any person
25   or public body for an account with a public utility as
26   defined in § 56-232; however, the custodian of such
27   records may disclose such account numbers at his
28   discretion, except where such disclosure is prohibited
29   by law. Access to such records shall be provided by the
30   custodian in accordance with this chapter by inspection
31   or by providing copies of the requested records, at the
32   option of the requester. The custodian may require the
33   requester to provide his name and legal address. The
34   custodian of such records shall take all necessary
35   precautions for their preservation and safekeeping.
36   B. A request for public records shall identify the
37   requested records with reasonable specificity. The
38   request need not make reference to this chapter in order
39   to invoke the provisions of this chapter or to impose the
40   time limits for response by a public body. Any public
41   body that is subject to this chapter and that is the
42   custodian of the requested records shall promptly, but in
43   all cases within five working days of receiving a request,
44   provide the requested records to the requester or make
45   one of the following responses in writing:
46   1. The requested records are being entirely withheld.
47   Such response shall identify with reasonable particularity
48   the volume and subject matter of withheld records, and
49   cite, as to each category of withheld records, the
50   specific Code section that authorizes the withholding of
51   the records.
52   2. The requested records are being provided in part and
53   are being withheld in part. Such response shall identify
54   with reasonable particularity the subject matter of
55   withheld portions, and cite, as to each category of
56   withheld records, the specific Code section that
57   authorizes the withholding of the records.
58   3. The requested records could not be found or do not
59   exist. However, if the public body that received the
60   request knows that another public body has the requested
61   records, the response shall include contact information
62   for the other public body.
63   4. It is not practically possible to provide the
64   requested records or to determine whether they are
65   available within the five-work-day period. Such response
66   shall specify the conditions that make a response
67   impossible. If the response is made within five working
68   days, the public body shall have an additional seven work
69   days or, in the case of a request for criminal
70   investigative files pursuant to § 2.2-3706.1, 60 work
71   days in which to provide one of the four preceding
72   responses.
73   C. Any public body may petition the appropriate court for
74   additional time to respond to a request for records when
75   the request is for an extraordinary volume of records or
76   requires an extraordinarily lengthy search, and a response
77   by the public body within the time required by this chapter
78   will prevent the public body from meeting its operational
79   responsibilities. Before proceeding with the petition,
80   however, the public body shall make reasonable efforts to
81   reach an agreement with the requester concerning the
82   production of the records requested.
83   D. Subject to the provisions of subsection G, no public
84   body shall be required to create a new record if the
85   record does not already exist. However, a public body may
86   abstract or summarize information under such terms and
87   conditions as agreed between the requester and the public
88   body.
89   E. Failure to respond to a request for records shall be
90   deemed a denial of the request and shall constitute a
91   violation of this chapter.
92   F. A public body may make reasonable charges not to
93   exceed its actual cost incurred in accessing, duplicating,
94   supplying, or searching for the requested records and
95   shall make all reasonable efforts to supply the requested
96   records at the lowest possible cost. No public body shall
97   impose any extraneous, intermediary, or surplus fees or
98   expenses to recoup the general costs associated with
99   creating or maintaining records or transacting the
100  general business of the public body. Any duplicating fee
101  charged by a public body shall not exceed the actual cost
102  of duplication. Prior to conducting a search for records,
103  the public body shall notify the requester in writing
104  that the public body may make reasonable charges not to
105  exceed its actual cost incurred in accessing, duplicating,
106  supplying, or searching for requested records and inquire
107  of the requester whether he would like to request a cost
108  estimate in advance of the supplying of the requested
109  records. The public body shall provide the requester with
110  a cost estimate if requested. If the public body receives
111  no response from the requester within 30 days of sending
112  the cost estimate, the request shall be deemed to be
113  withdrawn.
114  G. Public records maintained by a public body in an
115  electronic data processing system, computer database, or
116  any other structured collection of data shall be made
117  available to a requester at a reasonable cost, not to
118  exceed the actual cost in accordance with subsection F.
119  When electronic or other databases are combined or contain
120  exempt and nonexempt records, the public body may provide
121  access to the exempt records if not otherwise prohibited
122  by law, but shall provide access to the nonexempt records
123  as provided by this chapter. No public body shall be
124  required to produce records from an electronic database
125  in a format not regularly used by the public body.
126  However, the public body shall make reasonable efforts to
127  provide records in any format under such terms and
128  conditions as agreed between the requester and public
129  body, including the payment of reasonable costs.
130  H. In any case where a public body determines in advance
131  that charges for producing the requested records are
132  likely to exceed $200, the public body may, before
133  continuing to process the request, require the requester
134  to pay a deposit not to exceed the amount of the advance
135  determination. The deposit shall be credited toward the
136  final cost of supplying the requested records. The period
137  within which the public body shall respond under this
138  section shall be tolled for the amount of time that
139  elapses between notice of the advance determination and
140  the response of the requester.
141  I. Before processing a request for records, a public body
142  may require the requester to pay any amounts owed to the
143  public body for previous requests for records that remain
144  unpaid 30 days or more after billing.
145  J. In the event a public body has transferred possession
146  of public records to any entity, including but not limited
147  to any other public body, for storage, maintenance, or
148  archiving, the public body initiating the transfer of such
149  records shall remain the custodian of such records for
150  purposes of responding to requests for public records made
151  pursuant to this chapter and shall be responsible for
152  retrieving and supplying such public records to the
153  requester. In the event a public body has transferred
154  public records for storage, maintenance, or archiving and
155  such transferring public body is no longer in existence,
156  any public body that is a successor to the transferring
157  public body shall be deemed the custodian of such records.
158  In the event no successor entity exists, the entity in
159  possession of the public records shall be deemed the
160  custodian of the records for purposes of compliance with
161  this chapter, and shall retrieve and supply such records
162  to the requester. Nothing in this section shall be
163  construed to apply to records transferred to the Library
164  of Virginia for permanent archiving pursuant to the duties
165  imposed by the Virginia Public Records Act (§ 42.1-76 et
166  seq.). In accordance with § 42.1-79, the Library of
167  Virginia shall be the custodian of such permanently
168  archived records and shall be responsible for responding
169  to requests for such records made pursuant to this chapter.
